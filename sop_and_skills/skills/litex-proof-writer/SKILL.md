# litex-proof-writer

Write the mathematical proof idea first, then a natural Litex statement and
the shortest meaningful checked proof route. Preserve domains, premises and
the intended conclusion; do not hide a failed obligation behind a weaker goal.

- Read [writing and teaching](references/writing-and-teaching.md) for new source and learner help.
- Read [authoring error cards](references/authoring-error-cards.md) at the first actual stopping point.
- Read [proof style](references/proof-style.md) for a relevant mathematical move.
- Use [the session and journal workflow](references/litex-repl-try-workflow.md) for iterative work.
- Use [concept modeling](../litex-concept-modeler/SKILL.md) for an unsettled definition or reusable interface.
- Use [equality and inequality](../litex-equality-inequality/SKILL.md) for calculation cleanup.

Prefer direct facts for routine builtin-supported consequences. Retain genuine
witnesses, case splits, induction and reusable theorem interfaces. Never call a
proof containing unproved trust fully checkable. Keep a durable accepted prefix
and exact failure evidence; an ordinary failed statement retains prior context.

The [source guide](references/source-guide.md) preserves deeper mathematical,
dataset and repository-specific guidance. Its old command recipes are historical.
